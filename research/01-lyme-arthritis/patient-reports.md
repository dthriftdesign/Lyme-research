# Topic 1: Lyme and Arthritis — Patient Reports

## Methodology and a note on citation integrity

This file synthesizes **recurring patterns** reported across patient communities
listed in HANDOFF.md §7 — primarily r/Lyme, r/lymedisease, LymeNet Flash,
HealingWell's Lyme forum, and LymeDisease.org's community. Per the project's
quality bar, forum content is **paraphrased, never quoted at length, and never
attributed to a named user**.

**Integrity caveat:** This synthesis describes patterns that are well-attested
and repeatedly observable across these public communities. To avoid fabricating
precision, forum-excerpt records in `sources.json` link to **platform- or
search-level URLs**, not invented thread permalinks, and dates are given as
**approximate eras**. Where a future session verifies a specific thread, its
permalink should replace the platform-level link. Patterns are tagged by how
many independent posters typically describe them (rough counts only). This keeps
the file useful for pattern-recognition while staying honest about sourcing.

## Recurring Symptom Patterns

### Pattern: Migratory then knee-dominant large-joint swelling
- **Description:** Posters describe joint pain that "moves around" early
  (wrists, ankles, shoulders) before settling into one or both knees with
  visible, sometimes dramatic, swelling and stiffness more than pain. This
  closely matches the textbook large-joint, knee-predominant picture.
- **Platforms:** r/Lyme, LymeNet, HealingWell.
- **Approximate frequency:** common.
- **Relationship to literature:** confirms [T1-S002][T1-S013].
- **Source IDs:** [T1-F001].

### Pattern: Arthritis persists or recurs after "completing" antibiotics
- **Description:** A recurrent and emotionally charged theme — joint swelling
  that does not fully resolve, or returns, after one or two antibiotic courses.
  Patients frequently interpret this as ongoing infection and seek more
  antibiotics; many report a rheumatologist instead offering steroids/DMARDs.
- **Platforms:** r/Lyme, r/lymedisease, LymeDisease.org.
- **Approximate frequency:** common.
- **Relationship to literature:** matches the antibiotic-refractory phenomenon
  [T1-S001]; the patient *interpretation* (persistent infection) aligns with the
  minority view [T1-S010] and is contested by the mainstream post-infectious
  model [T1-S017][T1-S008].
- **Source IDs:** [T1-F002].

### Pattern: Dramatic relief from intra-articular steroids or synovectomy
- **Description:** Some posters report that joint aspiration + steroid injection,
  or eventually arthroscopic synovectomy, resolved a knee that years of
  antibiotics had not. Often framed with surprise.
- **Platforms:** r/Lyme, HealingWell.
- **Approximate frequency:** occasional.
- **Relationship to literature:** confirms the immune-mediated/refractory
  management pathway [T1-S001][T1-S017]; implicitly contradicts the pure-
  persistent-infection interpretation [T1-S010].
- **Source IDs:** [T1-F003].

### Pattern: "Herx"-style flares attributed to treatment
- **Description:** Reports of transient worsening of joint and systemic symptoms
  after starting antibiotics, labeled a Jarisch-Herxheimer-like reaction and
  read as proof of active infection.
- **Platforms:** r/Lyme, LymeNet.
- **Approximate frequency:** common.
- **Relationship to literature:** Jarisch-Herxheimer is documented in
  spirochetal disease but is **not** validated as a marker of *Lyme* treatment
  response; no literature link confirms it specifically tracks Lyme arthritis
  activity. Flagged as **no firm literature linkage**.
- **Source IDs:** [T1-F004].

### Pattern: Seronegative self-identification despite arthritis
- **Description:** A subset insist they have Lyme arthritis despite negative
  two-tier serology, often pursuing specialty labs. This conflicts sharply with
  the mainstream position that Lyme arthritis is essentially always strongly
  IgG-seropositive.
- **Platforms:** r/Lyme, LymeNet.
- **Approximate frequency:** occasional.
- **Relationship to literature:** contradicts [T1-S001][T1-S002] (negative IgG
  effectively excludes Lyme arthritis). Important tension: these posters may
  have non-Lyme arthritides being attributed to Lyme.
- **Source IDs:** [T1-F005].

## What patients describe that literature handles only partially

- **Weather/activity-linked knee flares** long after treatment — common in
  posts, under-characterized in literature (consistent with a mechanical/
  immune post-infectious joint rather than active infection).
- **Strong desire for an infectious explanation** over an autoimmune one — a
  social/psychological pattern that shapes treatment-seeking and helps explain
  the durability of the persistence debate at the patient level.
