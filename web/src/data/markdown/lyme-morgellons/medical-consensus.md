# Topic 2: Lyme and Morgellons — Medical Consensus

> Scope note: "Medical consensus" here means the dominant position in
> dermatology, psychiatry, and infectious disease. For Morgellons that consensus
> is itself a contested classification, so this file states it precisely and the
> dissenting borrelial-dermatitis hypothesis is developed in `non-consensus.md`.

## Overview

Morgellons disease (MD) is a controversial, self-identified condition whose
hallmark complaints are **crawling, biting, or stinging sensations**, the belief
that **fibers or filaments emerge from the skin**, and chronic non-healing skin
lesions, usually with fatigue, cognitive complaints, and joint pain. The
mainstream medical position, anchored by the large CDC study, is that Morgellons
is best understood as a presentation of **delusional infestation (DI)** — also
called delusional parasitosis — in which the "fibers" examined are
overwhelmingly textile (cellulose/cotton) and skin findings are consistent with
self-excoriation rather than an infectious dermopathy [T2-S001][T2-S003].

The Lyme connection enters because a vocal minority of patients and a small
group of researchers (Middelveen, Stricker) hold that Morgellons is a
**spirochetal/borrelial dermatitis** — a manifestation or complication of
*Borrelia* infection — and many Morgellons patients carry or seek a Lyme
diagnosis. That hypothesis is treated in `non-consensus.md`. This file documents
why mainstream medicine does not accept a *Borrelia* etiology.

## The defining mainstream evidence

### The CDC "unexplained dermopathy" study (Pearson et al., 2012)
A ~$600,000 CDC investigation enrolled 115 case-patients (Kaiser Permanente
Northern California). Findings [T2-S001]:
- No parasites or mycobacteria detected.
- Skin materials were predominantly **cellulose, consistent with cotton/textile
  origin** — not biological fibers produced by the body.
- No single underlying infectious or environmental cause identified.
- Neuropsychiatric findings and prevalence of drug detection were notable;
  the syndrome resembled DI clinically.
This remains the largest, most rigorous population study and is the backbone of
the consensus position.

### Histology of patient-provided fibers (Hylwa et al., 2011)
Independent histologic examination of skin biopsies and patient-supplied
"specimens" in delusional-infestation patients found cutaneous debris,
environmental detritus, or textile/plant material — **no objective evidence of
infestation** [T2-S003]. This is the mainstream interpretive frame: the fibers
are external contaminants, not endogenous.

## How mainstream medicine classifies Morgellons

- **Delusional infestation (DI)** is a well-characterized psychodermatologic
  condition: a fixed false belief of infestation despite contrary evidence,
  often with the "matchbox/specimen sign" (patients bring collected fibers)
  [T2-S002]. Mainstream dermatology maps most Morgellons presentations onto DI,
  sometimes secondary to other psychiatric or medical conditions (including
  stimulant use — see Topic 3) [T2-S002].
- Importantly, the DI framing does **not** mean symptoms are imaginary: the
  **formication** (crawling/biting sensation) is a real neurologic sensory
  experience, and the skin lesions are real (from scratching/picking). The
  dispute is about **cause**, specifically whether fibers are endogenous and
  whether *Borrelia* drives the syndrome [T2-S002][T2-S011].

## Diagnostic Approach (mainstream)

- Rule out genuine infestation, primary dermatologic disease, and organic causes
  of pruritus/formication (thyroid, renal, hepatic, B12, neuropathy, stimulant
  or dopaminergic-drug effects).
- Examine submitted specimens; textile fibers support DI [T2-S003].
- Screen for stimulant use and psychiatric comorbidity [T2-S002].
- A careful, non-confrontational therapeutic alliance is emphasized because
  patients typically reject a psychiatric framing [T2-S011].

## Treatment Consensus (mainstream)

- Multidisciplinary psychodermatology care; treat comorbid depression/anxiety.
- Second-generation antipsychotics (e.g., risperidone, olanzapine, aripiprazole)
  have the best evidence for DI, though acceptance is the main barrier
  [T2-S002].
- Mainstream guidance does **not** support long-term antibiotics for Morgellons,
  and notes that perceived antibiotic benefit is confounded by placebo,
  expectation, and the natural waxing/waning of the condition [T2-S011].

## Open Questions Within Mainstream

- Is there a **subgroup** with a genuine organic dermatosis misfiled under DI?
  The recent literature calls for standardized criteria and controlled trials
  precisely because the category is heterogeneous [T2-S011].
- The relationship between Morgellons, **stimulant-induced formication**, and
  primary DI is not cleanly resolved (see Topic 3).
- The high rate of self-reported Lyme/tick-borne diagnoses in Morgellons cohorts
  is unexplained by the consensus and is the crux of the dispute.
