# Topic 6: Lyme, Parasitic Co-infection, and Suicide — Insights

> **Crisis resources.** US — call/text 988; UK — Samaritans 116 123;
> international — findahelpline.com. Public-health framing; no methods detail.

## Emergent Patterns

### Pattern: The mechanism is mainstream; only the attribution is contested
As in Topic 4, the infection-suicide hypothesis does **not** need a novel
mechanism. Inflammation → kynurenine → quinolinic-acid excitotoxicity →
suicidality is established mainstream neuroscience [T6-S004][T6-S005]. Bransfield's
contribution is to argue *Borrelia*/co-infection feeds that same pathway
[T6-S001]. So the honest scientific question is narrow and tractable: **does
tick-borne infection measurably elevate kynurenine-pathway markers and suicide
risk above the chronic-illness baseline?** — not "can infection affect mood?"
(yes).

### Pattern: Two independent risk pathways converge
Patient reports and literature both suggest **two additive routes**: (1) a
*biological* route (neuroinflammation/quinolinic acid) [T6-S004][T6-S005], and
(2) a *psychosocial* route (chronic pain, disability, and especially **medical
invalidation**) [T6-S006][T6-F001]. Co-infection severity [T6-S007] amplifies
both. The psychosocial route is the **least controversial and most modifiable** —
believing patients and linking them to care is a concrete intervention that does
not depend on resolving the infection-attribution debate.

### Pattern: "This isn't my normal self" as a candidate clinical signal
The recurring patient descriptor that the mood change feels *alien to baseline*
[T6-F002] is phenomenologically distinct from typical primary depression and fits
an acute neuroinflammatory insult better. It is soft evidence, but it is a
specific, falsifiable observation that could be operationalized in research.

## Hypotheses Worth Investigating
- **H1 (grounded, testable):** Compare kynurenine-pathway/quinolinic-acid markers
  and validated suicide-risk scales in active Lyme/co-infection patients vs.
  matched chronic-illness controls. This directly tests the infection-specific
  claim against the chronic-illness baseline [T6-S001][T6-S004][T6-S005].
- **H2 (grounded, actionable now):** Invalidation is a modifiable risk factor
  [T6-F001][T6-S006]; clinician-communication and validation interventions could
  be studied as suicide-prevention measures in contested chronic illness —
  independent of the etiology debate.
- **H3 (speculative):** The "alien to baseline" mood-change descriptor [T6-F002]
  could be developed into a screening item distinguishing neuroinflammatory from
  primary mood change.

## Contradictions and Tensions
- **Infection-specific suicide signal** [T6-S001] **vs. chronic-illness
  confounding** [T6-S006] — the core unresolved attribution problem.
- **Under-recognition risk** (delaying care by missing it) **vs. over-attribution
  risk** (delaying psychiatric care by blaming infection) — a genuine clinical
  double bind.
- **Aggression/homicidality claims** [T6-S002] are `speculative` and risk
  stigmatizing patients; the project flags them as such rather than amplifying
  them.
