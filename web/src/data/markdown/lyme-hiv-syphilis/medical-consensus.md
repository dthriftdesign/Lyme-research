# Topic 7: Lyme, HIV, and Syphilis — Medical Consensus

> Scope: the real biomedical intersections among *Borrelia burgdorferi* (Lyme),
> *Treponema pallidum* (syphilis), and HIV — chiefly **spirochetal biology and
> the "great imitator" parallel**, **serologic cross-reactivity**, and
> **immunosuppression effects on diagnosis/course**. Fringe "engineered-triad"
> claims are addressed in `non-consensus.md`.

## Overview

Three distinct relationships are well-supported:

1. **Lyme ↔ syphilis (biological homology).** Both are **spirochetal** infections
   with staged disease (early localized → disseminated → late/CNS), tissue
   persistence over years, neurotropism, and antibiotic susceptibility. Lyme was
   dubbed **"the new great imitator"** by analogy to syphilis, the original great
   imitator [T7-S004][T7-S005].
2. **Lyme ↔ syphilis (serologic cross-reactivity).** Antibodies cross-react
   between *Borrelia* and *Treponema*, causing **false-positive treponemal tests
   (FTA-Abs) in Lyme** and confounding both diagnoses [T7-S001][T7-S002][T7-S003].
3. **HIV ↔ Lyme (immunosuppression & diagnosis).** HIV can alter the
   presentation, serologic response, and possibly course of Lyme; conversely,
   tick-borne infections complicate the immunosuppressed host [T7-S006].

## Established Mechanisms

### Spirochetal homology and the "great imitator"
*Borrelia* and *Treponema* are both spirochetes capable of immune evasion,
long-term tissue persistence, and CNS invasion. **Neuroborreliosis and
neurosyphilis overlap clinically** — cranial neuropathies, meningitis,
radiculopathy, cognitive/psychiatric decline — and each can mimic many other
neurological diseases [T7-S004][T7-S005]. The staged natural history (skin →
dissemination → late CNS) is strikingly parallel.

### Serologic cross-reactivity
Shared spirochetal antigens (including flagellar and other conserved proteins)
mean:
- Lyme patients can show **isolated positive FTA-Abs** (one study: ~43% of Lyme
  sera) — a false-positive *syphilis* signal [T7-S002].
- Conversely, syphilis sera can react in Lyme IFA/ELISA assays [T7-S001].
- This is why a positive Lyme or treponemal test must be interpreted with the
  other spirochetosis in mind, and why **non-treponemal tests (RPR/VDRL) and
  two-tier Lyme testing** plus clinical context are needed to disambiguate
  [T7-S003].

### HIV effects on Lyme
In HIV co-infection, Lyme can present atypically (e.g., persistent erythema
migrans, meningoradiculitis) with **discordant or attenuated serology** —
immunofluorescence negative while ELISA/Western blot positive — paralleling how
syphilis behaves differently in HIV [T7-S006]. *B. burgdorferi* itself can cause
**transient immunosuppression** and has been shown to enhance *Babesia*
transmission, a further immune cross-talk [T7-S007]. (Detailed WBC/immune
mechanisms are Topic 8.)

### Cross-reactive false positives beyond syphilis
Babesiosis (the parasitic co-infection of Topic 5) has been reported to cause
**false-positive HIV serology**, illustrating how tick-borne infections
reverberate through unrelated serologic assays [T7-S007].

## Diagnostic Approach (mainstream)

- A positive **treponemal test** in a Lyme-endemic patient with no syphilis risk
  should prompt confirmation (RPR/VDRL, clinical history) before diagnosing
  syphilis — and vice versa [T7-S002][T7-S003].
- In HIV patients, expect possible **serologic discordance**; use CSF analysis
  and direct/molecular methods where indicated [T7-S006].
- Maintain syphilis and HIV testing in the differential for neurologic syndromes
  attributed to Lyme — the great-imitator cuts both ways.

## Treatment Consensus (mainstream)

- Each infection has its own evidence-based regimen (Lyme per 2020 guideline;
  syphilis per CDC STI guidelines with stage-appropriate penicillin;
  neurosyphilis with IV penicillin; HIV with ART).
- Co-infection requires treating each; HIV immune status influences monitoring
  and follow-up [T7-S006].

## Open Questions Within Mainstream

- **Does HIV alter Lyme course/treatment response** as it does for syphilis?
  Explicitly flagged as unresolved in the HIV-Lyme literature [T7-S006].
- **How often are isolated positive treponemal tests actually Lyme** in endemic
  areas? Under-appreciated source of syphilis misdiagnosis [T7-S002].
- The molecular basis of cross-reactivity (which shared epitopes) is partly but
  not fully mapped [T7-S003].
