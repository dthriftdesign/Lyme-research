# Topic 11: How Lyme Is Transmitted — Non-Consensus / Minority Research

> Flags: `mainstream` · `minority-research` · `fringe` · `historical` ·
> `speculative`. Inclusion is not endorsement. This topic is included precisely
> because the project's mandate is to investigate, not dismiss, minority claims —
> and because the **congenital-syphilis analogy** gives the vertical-transmission
> question genuine scientific motivation.

## Overview

The minority position holds that *Borrelia* is transmitted **more readily than
the tick-only paradigm allows** — specifically **vertically (mother to child, in
utero)** as a real and underappreciated route, and **sexually** between partners.
The strongest version of the argument is not "ticks don't matter" but "**a
spirochete closely related to syphilis behaves like syphilis**." This file states
that case at its strongest and then flags where the evidence is and isn't.

## The congenital-syphilis analogy — the serious scientific core — `minority-research`/`speculative`

This is the most important and most defensible idea in the topic. *Treponema
pallidum* (syphilis) and *Borrelia burgdorferi* (Lyme) are **both spirochetes**,
and Lyme has long been called "the new great imitator" by direct analogy to
syphilis (Topic 7 [T7-S004]). *T. pallidum* is transmitted **both sexually and
transplacentally** — **congenital syphilis is a fully established, major clinical
entity** [T11-S008]. So the question "if one pathogenic spirochete crosses the
placenta and transmits sexually, can the other?" is **biologically reasonable**,
not fringe.

- **What supports the analogy:** shared spirochetal biology, documented placental
  crossing of *B. burgdorferi* [T11-S002], and detection of *Borrelia* in genital
  secretions [T11-S006].
- **What weakens it (stated honestly):** *T. pallidum* is an obligate human
  pathogen that sustains prolonged **spirochetemia** and genital-mucosa shedding —
  exactly the biology that enables sexual and congenital spread. *B. burgdorferi*
  is **tick-adapted**, produces only brief, low-level bloodstream presence, and
  localizes to skin/joints/nervous tissue. Being a spirochete is **necessary but
  not sufficient** for syphilis-like transmission. The analogy generates a strong
  hypothesis; it does not prove the conclusion.

Status: the analogy is a legitimate `minority-research` hypothesis-generator;
the specific claim that Lyme transmits like syphilis is `speculative`.

## Vertical / congenital transmission — `minority-research`

The minority view (ILADS, congenital-Lyme advocates, and a growing patient
literature) holds congenital Lyme is **real and underdiagnosed**, explaining
multi-generational family clusters and "no-bite" cases [T11-S009]. Evidence:
- **Documented placental crossing and adverse outcomes**, especially untreated
  [T11-S002][T11-S004] — this part overlaps with mainstream.
- **Observational signals** of higher adverse outcomes in maternal Lyme
  [T11-S004], and a recent push to advance the science of perinatal transmission
  [T11-S009].
- **The honest gap:** demonstrating that a child has a *clinically significant,
  congenitally-acquired Borrelia infection* (vs. transient transfer, maternal
  antibody, or independent later exposure) is hard, and the controlled data
  needed to define a syndrome do not yet exist [T11-S003][T11-S005]. That is why
  mainstream removed the ICD category — *insufficient evidence*, which is not the
  same as *evidence of absence*.

## Sexual transmission — `fringe`

Middelveen, Stricker and colleagues reported **culturing/detecting *Borrelia* in
human semen and vaginal secretions**, including in partner couples, and argued
this "challenges the tickborne paradigm" [T11-S006][T11-S007]. Assessment:
- The detection claims are **single-group, contamination-vulnerable, and
  unreplicated**, mirroring the same group's Morgellons work (Topic 2).
- Detecting DNA/organisms in secretions is **not** the same as demonstrating
  **infectious sexual transmission causing disease** — the epidemiology (no
  STI-like clustering, partners not at elevated risk) argues against it
  [T11-S001][T11-S005].
- Status: `fringe` as a route of clinically significant transmission; the
  detection observation is `minority-research` and would need independent,
  blinded replication.

## The MS / familial-autoimmunity thread — `speculative`

A related minority idea: that **familial autoimmune disease (e.g., multiple
sclerosis)** in relatives of Lyme patients reflects undiagnosed hereditary/
congenital *Borrelia* infection. *Borrelia* genuinely can **mimic MS**
(neuroborreliosis is an MS differential) and there is a **contested association**
between *Borrelia* serology and MS [T11-S010]. But:
- MS mimicry/association is **not** evidence that MS *is* Lyme, nor that Lyme is
  hereditary.
- Family clustering of autoimmune disease has many explanations (shared genes,
  shared environment/tick exposure) besides vertical infection.
Status: `speculative`. The MS link is a real, investigable mimicry/comorbidity
question; using a relative's MS as proof of hereditary Lyme is an
over-interpretation the honest position cannot endorse.

## Honest epistemic assessment

- **Tick-borne transmission** — `mainstream`, dominant [T11-S001].
- **Transplacental crossing of the spirochete can occur (rare, esp. untreated)**
  — `mainstream` [T11-S002][T11-S003].
- **A defined congenital Lyme *syndrome*** — not established; `minority-research`
  (insufficient, not disproven) [T11-S005][T11-S009].
- **Sexual transmission causing disease** — `fringe`/unsupported [T11-S001][T11-S006].
- **Hereditary Lyme inferred from a relative's MS** — `speculative` [T11-S010].
