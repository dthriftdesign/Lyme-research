# Topic 3: Lyme, Morgellons, and Amphetamines — Medical Consensus

> Scope: the intersection of stimulant pharmacology, formication, and the
> Morgellons/Lyme phenomenology. Harm-reduction framing where relevant. The
> competing "organic borrelial dermatitis" reading lives in `non-consensus.md`.

## Overview

This topic sits at a documented and clinically important crossroads: **chronic
amphetamine/methamphetamine (and cocaine) use reliably produces formication** —
the sensation of insects crawling on or under the skin — together with
compulsive skin-picking and the fixed belief of infestation. In the
addiction-medicine and psychiatry literature this is called variously "meth
mites," "coke bugs" (Magnan's sign), or, formally, **stimulant-induced
delusional infestation / tactile hallucinosis** [T3-S001][T3-S002]. Because the
phenomenology is essentially identical to Morgellons (fibers/bugs, lesions,
self-documentation), stimulant use is the single most important **differential
diagnosis and confound** for Morgellons, and a plausible explanation for a
subgroup of cases [T3-S002][T3-S004].

The Lyme element is indirect: patients who attribute these sensations to chronic
Lyme/Morgellons may have undisclosed stimulant use, and the CDC Morgellons study
specifically noted detectable drugs in a notable share of its cohort [T3-S005].

## Established Mechanisms

### Dopaminergic tactile hallucinosis
Amphetamines and methamphetamine increase synaptic dopamine (enhanced release,
blocked reuptake). Excess striatal/mesolimbic dopamine signaling is the leading
mechanism for stimulant psychosis, in which **tactile and visual hallucinations
are disproportionately prominent** compared with schizophrenia [T3-S003]. The
same dopaminergic logic explains why **dopamine-agonist therapy** (e.g., in
Parkinson's disease) and dopaminergic states can produce delusional infestation,
and why **antipsychotics (D2 blockers)** treat it [T3-S004].

### Formication as a real sensory event
Formication is a genuine somatosensory experience, not "imagined." Chronic
stimulant use can cause peripheral and central sensory changes plus the
dopaminergic central misattribution that converts paresthesia into a conviction
of infestation [T3-S001]. The skin lesions are then real, produced by repetitive
picking/excoriation, and prone to secondary infection [T3-S001].

### Epidemiology of stimulant psychosis
Roughly 26–46% of methamphetamine-dependent individuals experience
stimulant-associated psychosis at some point, with tactile hallucinations
notably more common than in primary psychotic disorders [T3-S003]. Formication
("meth mites") is a frequent complaint in heavy daily users [T3-S001].

## Diagnostic Approach (mainstream)

- In any patient presenting with formication, fibers, or "infestation,"
  **screen for stimulant use** (amphetamine prescription and illicit, cocaine,
  decongestants, and dopaminergic drugs) [T3-S002][T3-S004].
- Examine specimens (textile fibers support DI) and assess for excoriation.
- Distinguish **primary** delusional infestation from **secondary** (drug-,
  medical-, or psychiatric-induced) because management differs [T3-S004].
- Note that prescription stimulant use for ADHD can also precipitate formication
  at high doses — relevant to harm reduction and dose review.

## Treatment Consensus (mainstream)

- **Stop or reduce the stimulant** — formication and infestation beliefs often
  remit with abstinence; this is both diagnostic and therapeutic [T3-S001].
- Treat the substance-use disorder; harm-reduction support (safer-use, wound
  care, infection prevention) for those not ready to stop.
- Antipsychotics for persistent secondary DI; treat comorbid mood/anxiety
  [T3-S004].
- Wound care for excoriations; manage secondary skin infection.

## Open Questions Within Mainstream

- **What fraction of self-identified Morgellons cases are stimulant-related?**
  Undisclosed use makes this hard to quantify; the CDC drug-detection finding is
  suggestive but not definitive [T3-S005].
- **Persistence after abstinence:** some patients retain infestation beliefs long
  after stopping — is this a separate primary DI, sensitized circuitry, or
  unmasked underlying disease?
- **Prescription-stimulant dose thresholds** for formication are
  under-characterized.
