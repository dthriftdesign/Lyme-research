# Topic 3: Lyme, Morgellons, and Amphetamines — Insights

> Emergent-pattern layer. Hypotheses labeled as such.

## Emergent Patterns

### Pattern: One symptom, two communities, zero overlap in attribution
The most striking finding is structural: **the same somatosensory event**
(formication + skin-picking + "things in the skin") is described in stimulant
harm-reduction forums as an obvious, reversible drug effect [T3-F001][T3-F003]
and in Lyme/Morgellons forums as proof of chronic infection [T3-F002], with
almost no cross-reference between them. The communities use different causal
vocabularies for an identical phenomenon. This is a near-perfect natural
experiment in attribution: identical percept, opposite explanatory frame, driven
by community context and disclosure norms.

### Pattern: The differential diagnosis is strong but structurally hidden
Stimulant DI is a textbook differential for Morgellons [T3-S002][T3-S004], yet
the very communities most invested in an infectious explanation are the least
likely to surface stimulant history (stigma). So the strongest competing
explanation is also the one clinicians are least likely to hear — a systematic
blind spot, not a knowledge gap.

### Pattern: "Reversible with abstinence" is the cleanest available test
Unlike the Morgellons filament dispute (Topic 2), this topic has a built-in
falsifier: stimulant-caused formication should resolve with sustained abstinence
[T3-S001][T3-F003]. Symptom resolution after stopping is strong evidence for a
stimulant cause; persistence despite verified abstinence points elsewhere
(primary DI or organic disease). This is clinically actionable.

## Hypotheses Worth Investigating
- **H1 (grounded, testable):** Systematic, consenting toxicology + medication
  review in a Morgellons cohort would estimate the stimulant-attributable
  fraction. The CDC drug-detection signal [T3-S005] suggests it is non-trivial;
  no one has measured it directly since.
- **H2 (speculative):** In patients with both tick-borne infection and stimulant
  use, neuroinflammatory and dopaminergic mechanisms may be **additive** for
  tactile hallucinosis — untested, but mechanistically coherent.
- **H3 (grounded):** An abstinence-challenge protocol could prospectively
  separate stimulant-driven from non-drug Morgellons, partitioning the
  heterogeneous category the 2025 review flagged (Topic 2 [T2-S011]).

## Contradictions and Tensions
- **Patient infection-attribution** [T3-F002] vs. **well-evidenced stimulant
  mechanism** [T3-S001][T3-S003] for the identical symptom.
- **Minority claim that the overlap is a dismissal tactic** [T3-S006] vs.
  **legitimate differential-diagnostic necessity** [T3-S002][T3-S004] — both
  contain truth; neither licenses ignoring the other.
