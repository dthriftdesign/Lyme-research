# Topic 4: Lyme and OCD — Non-Consensus / Minority Research

> Flags: `mainstream` · `minority-research` · `fringe` · `historical` ·
> `speculative`. Inclusion is not endorsement.

## Overview

The minority position, advanced most prominently by **Robert Bransfield** and the
ILADS-adjacent neuropsychiatry literature, holds that tick-borne infection is a
**substantial, under-recognized cause of psychiatric illness** — including
OCD-spectrum symptoms — via immune/inflammatory and autoimmune mechanisms, and
that treating the infection can resolve the psychiatric presentation [T4-S004].
This sits on a spectrum from defensible (`minority-research`) to overreaching
(`speculative`/`fringe`) depending on how strong a causal/clinical claim is made.

## The neuropsychiatric-Lyme thesis (Bransfield) — `minority-research`

Bransfield argues that Lyme/tick-borne disease produces a gradually developing
spectrum of neuropsychiatric symptoms through "psychoimmunology" — cytokines,
microglial activation, autoimmunity, and metabolic effects — and that OCD,
intrusive thoughts, anxiety, and even aggression/suicidality (see Topic 6) can be
infection-driven [T4-S004]. The peer-reviewed-but-partisan character (published
in lower-impact venues, ILADS-aligned) places it in `minority-research`. The
core mechanistic claim — infection→neuroinflammation→psychiatric symptoms — is
biologically mainstream in principle; the contested part is its **frequency,
specificity to OCD, and reversibility with antibiotics**.

## Case-report evidence — `fringe`/`speculative`

Multiple case reports describe OCD or PANS-like presentations with serologic
evidence of Lyme that improved after antibiotics — e.g., a young patient meeting
PANDAS criteria with Lyme serology, and an adult with OCD whose symptoms remitted
after penicillin [T4-S008]. As a class, case reports are `fringe`/`speculative`
for causal inference: susceptible to coincidence, placebo, publication bias, and
serologic false positives. They are genuinely hypothesis-generating and worth
taking seriously as such — not as proof.

## "Lyme-triggered PANS" — `speculative`

The strongest mechanistic bridge is extending the **PANDAS/PANS** autoimmune
basal-ganglia model [T4-S005] from streptococcus to *Borrelia*. Some clinicians
diagnose "Lyme-/tick-borne-triggered PANS." Status: `speculative` — biologically
coherent (other infections including *Mycoplasma* are proposed PANS triggers),
but anti-neuronal autoantibody evidence specific to *Borrelia*-associated OCD has
not been established the way it has (contentiously even there) for streptococcus.

## Why mainstream is cautious (stated fairly)

- **Base rates:** OCD is common; Lyme exposure is common in endemic areas;
  co-occurrence is expected by chance.
- **Serologic false positives** drive over-diagnosis when Lyme is tested in
  low-pretest-probability psychiatric patients.
- **Antibiotic "response"** in uncontrolled cases is confounded by placebo, ERP,
  SSRIs, and natural OCD fluctuation.
- The retreatment-trial literature (Topic 1 [T1-S014]) argues against prolonged
  antibiotics for persistent symptom syndromes generally.

## Honest epistemic assessment

- **Neuroborreliosis can cause neuropsychiatric symptoms** — `mainstream`
  [T4-S002][T4-S007].
- **Infection-triggered autoimmune OCD exists as a category (PANDAS/PANS)** —
  `mainstream` for streptococcus [T4-S005], itself still debated.
- **Borrelia specifically triggering autoimmune OCD** — `speculative`, plausible,
  unproven [T4-S004][T4-S008].
- **Routine antibiotic treatment of OCD attributed to chronic Lyme** —
  unsupported; `fringe` as a general practice.

## Named researchers

Robert Bransfield (neuropsychiatric Lyme; `minority-research`); Brian Fallon
(Columbia; rigorous, more mainstream neuropsychiatric-Lyme researcher); the
PANDAS/PANS lineage (Swedo and successors) supplies the mechanistic template.
