# Topic 4: Lyme and OCD — Insights

> Emergent-pattern layer. Hypotheses labeled as such.

## Emergent Patterns

### Pattern: The mechanism is mainstream; only the pathogen is in question
The infection→autoimmune-basal-ganglia→OCD pathway is *accepted* science for
streptococcus (PANDAS/PANS) [T4-S005]. The Lyme-OCD claim does not require a new
mechanism — it requires showing *Borrelia* can use the **existing** one. This
reframes the debate productively: the question is not "can infection cause OCD?"
(yes) but "is there *Borrelia*-specific anti-neuronal autoimmunity?" — a concrete,
testable target rather than a vague "chronic Lyme" appeal.

### Pattern: This topic shares its causal skeleton with Lyme arthritis
Topic 1's vindicated story — *Borrelia* infection triggering identified
anti-self responses (ECGF, Annexin A2) that drive disease after the organism is
controlled [T1-S004] — is the **same template** the Lyme-OCD hypothesis needs in
the brain. Lyme arthritis proves *Borrelia* can induce organ-specific
autoimmunity in humans; that is the single strongest indirect support for taking
Lyme-OCD seriously, and it is rarely cited in that debate.

### Pattern: Directionality confound is the killer for clean inference
Patient reports show OCD can both **follow** Lyme and **target** Lyme/Morgellons
fears [T4-F004]. Without onset-timing and anti-neuronal-antibody data, antibiotic
"responses" can't distinguish infection-driven OCD from coincidence or the
illness merely supplying obsessional content. This is why case reports
[T4-S008] cannot settle it.

## Hypotheses Worth Investigating
- **H1 (grounded, testable):** Apply the PANDAS anti-neuronal-antibody assays
  (anti-basal-ganglia antibodies, CaMKII activation) to *Borrelia*-associated
  acute OCD cohorts. If positive and antibiotic/immunotherapy-responsive, it
  would elevate Lyme-OCD from `speculative` toward `minority-research`.
- **H2 (speculative):** The same HLA-restricted autoimmunity that predicts
  antibiotic-refractory Lyme arthritis [T1-S003] might mark a neuro-autoimmune
  subgroup — a cross-organ susceptibility hypothesis no one has tested.
- **H3 (grounded):** Prospective onset-timing registries (psychiatric symptoms
  vs. documented infection vs. treatment) would address the directionality
  confound case reports cannot.

## Contradictions and Tensions
- **Biologically plausible mechanism** [T4-S005] **vs. weak human Lyme-specific
  evidence** [T4-S008].
- **Patient antibiotic-response reports** [T4-F003] **vs. mainstream caution and
  confounding** [T4-S007].
- **Bransfield's broad neuropsychiatric claims** [T4-S004] **vs. base-rate and
  false-positive critiques** — the gap between "infection can affect the brain"
  (true) and "this patient's OCD is infectious" (usually unprovable).
